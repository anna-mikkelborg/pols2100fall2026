Recap

  • Scientific research starts with a theory
  • Really good theories are hard to come by
  • Theories cannot be tested; only hypotheses can
  • Doing so requires operationalization of key concepts into usable measures

Kellstedt and Whitten

Common pitfalls in the research process

An overly-broad research question

  • Don’t set yourself up for failure
  • Good rule of thumb is to focus on effects of causes rather than causes of effects
    • Let your independent variable lead the way

“Does experiencing famine (IV) increase or decrease the probability of a country going to war (DV)?”

“Why do countries go to war (DV)?”

Questionable operationalization

  • Making sure the measurement fits the concept can be tricky
  • Need to be able to make a strong case that your hypothesis tests actually tell us something about your theory
  • e.g., a 1956-2026 price increase doesn’t mean much until you adjust for inflation
  • e.g., incumbent party vote share (have to account for presence/absence of third parties)

Ignoring alternative explanations

  • Observational equivalence: multiple theories that produce the same observable outcomes
  • e.g., issue voting
    • It’s not that voters change their voting behavior to match their issue preferences…
    • …it’s often that they change their issue preferences to match those of the politician they’re voting for!
  • When possible, design hypothesis tests that can distinguish between all plausible explanations

Over-claiming

  • Firmly establishing a causal relationship is extremely hard
  • Sometimes the best we can do is gather a ton of suggestive evidence
  • Be transparent about the limitations of your tests!
  • Science is iterative

p-hacking

  • Tweaking data or statistical tests to get a preferred result
    • e.g., finding post-hoc justifications for focusing on a subset of the data
    • e.g., trying different model specifications to see which one shows the strongest relationship
  • More subtle than it sounds!
  • Pre-registration helps

Ethical issues

  • We are often gathering data on individual human beings
  • Don’t do unethical things to them!
    • Don’t coerce or pressure people to participate
    • Don’t invade participants’ privacy
    • Don’t put participants in danger
    • Don’t make them uncomfortable for no reason

Ethical issues, continued

  • Protect participants’ identities
    • Data should be anonymized as quickly as possible
    • Identifying information should be stored securely
  • Institutional Review Boards exist for this reason
